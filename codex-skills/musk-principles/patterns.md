# Patterns

## First-Principles Rebuild

**When to use**: costs are high, timelines look impossible, or industry consensus feels lazy.

**How**:
1. State the desired outcome.
2. Strip away inherited process and vendor assumptions.
3. Identify fundamental constraints.
4. Rebuild a solution from materials, physics, economics, and logic.
5. Compare the rebuilt path with the conventional path.

**Trade-offs**: can reveal breakthroughs, but requires real technical understanding.

## Delete Before Optimize

**When to use**: systems, products, meetings, specs, and workflows have accumulated complexity.

**How**:
1. Question every requirement.
2. Delete parts or steps.
3. Restore only what proves necessary.
4. Simplify the remaining flow.
5. Accelerate and automate last.

**Trade-offs**: deletion feels risky; it is the fastest way to expose what actually matters.

## Factory-as-Product Review

**When to use**: hardware, operations, logistics, or any product that must scale.

**How**:
1. Map the production flow.
2. Find the slowest or most fragile step.
3. Redesign tooling, layout, and feedback loops.
4. Measure throughput, cost, quality, and cycle time.
5. Treat manufacturing improvements as product improvements.

**Trade-offs**: less glamorous than prototypes; often creates the real moat.

## Mission Filter

**When to use**: choosing projects, hiring, fundraising, or cutting scope.

**How**:
1. Name the civilizational or customer benefit.
2. Ask whether the work contributes more than it consumes.
3. Remove status-driven work.
4. Choose the path that compounds the mission.

**Trade-offs**: narrows options; increases coherence.

## Urgency Loop

**When to use**: a team is moving slowly or waiting for sequential certainty.

**How**:
1. Set a near-term milestone.
2. Run independent tasks in parallel.
3. Put decision makers near the work.
4. Accept fast learning from failed attempts.
5. Repeat with shorter feedback cycles.

**Trade-offs**: can burn people out if urgency is not paired with clear priorities.

## Talent Density Reset

**When to use**: teams are large but output is weak.

**How**:
1. Identify the builders who create core value.
2. Remove unnecessary coordination layers.
3. Give direct ownership to high-signal contributors.
4. Keep feedback honest and fast.

**Trade-offs**: culturally uncomfortable; improves speed and accountability.
