## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Useful Work Over Vanity
Use this when prioritizing career, projects, or company strategy. Prefer work that makes a measurable contribution to humanity, customers, infrastructure, capability, or knowledge. Avoid status games that look impressive but do not increase useful output.

### Purpose as Operating System
Treat a mission as a constraint that selects projects, people, pace, and trade-offs. A serious mission is not motivational copy; it determines what pain you are willing to endure and what compromises you reject.

### First-Principles Thinking
Use first principles when assumptions seem expensive, inherited, or artificially constrained. Break a problem into physical, economic, or logical fundamentals; rebuild from what must be true rather than from how the industry usually works.

### Limit Thinking
Ask where the true physical, technical, or economic limit is. If the current plan is far from the limit, the bottleneck is likely process, belief, incentives, organization, or courage rather than reality.

### Engineering Creates Leverage
Engineering is treated as the engine of civilization and company advantage. Use engineering quality as a strategic variable, not a back-office function. Products, factories, software, energy systems, and rockets win through technical capability.

### Detail Ownership
Leaders must understand details deeply enough to remove constraints, challenge excuses, and make fast trade-offs. Do not manage only through summaries when the system is failing.

### Builder Culture
Hire people who build, test, ship, and take responsibility. Protect direct feedback, high standards, and hands-on problem solving. Remove politics, vague ownership, and comfort with mediocrity.

### Algorithm for Work
For hard execution, use the recurring simplification loop:
1. Question every requirement.
2. Delete any part or process that is not necessary.
3. Simplify and optimize what remains.
4. Accelerate cycle time.
5. Automate only after the process is correct.

### Urgency as Strategy
Speed is both offense and defense. Compress schedules, run parallel paths, make decisions close to the work, and treat time waste as a design flaw. Urgency is useful only when paired with technical clarity and feedback.

### Manufacturing Is the Product
For hardware or operations, the factory, supply chain, tooling, and production process are part of the product. A brilliant prototype is incomplete until manufacturing can scale it reliably and affordably.

### Founder Mode
Use founder mode when building from zero or rescuing a fragile company: concentrate responsibility, stay close to customers and technical work, reallocate resources quickly, and endure short-term discomfort for long-term mission survival.

### Future-Back Company Building
Choose companies by asking what future civilization needs: sustainable energy, transport, space access, AI safety, human-machine interfaces, and abundance. Work backward from civilizational bottlenecks.

### Abundance Orientation
Scarcity is not fixed. Technology can reduce costs, increase capability, and expand access. Use this frame when evaluating markets that seem constrained by energy, compute, transportation, knowledge, or manufacturing.

### Existential Risk Awareness
Long-term optimism should coexist with serious risk awareness. Evaluate AI, energy, population, conflict, regulation, and planetary fragility as design constraints for civilization.

### Multi-Planetary Ambition
The Mars / multi-planetary frame is a forcing function for scale, resilience, and inspiration. It asks whether work expands the long-term survival and possibility space of life.
