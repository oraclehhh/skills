# Patterns

## 良知照面

**When to use**: when a decision is morally blurry but you suspect the right direction is already known.

**How**:
1. State the concrete affair.
2. Name the first honest moral signal.
3. Identify the desire, fear, or convenience that covers it.
4. Take the smallest action that aligns with the signal.

**Trade-offs**: guards against overthinking; can be abused if impulse is mistaken for 良知.

## 知行合一 Audit

**When to use**: when someone says they understand but repeatedly does not act.

**How**:
1. Ask what they claim to know.
2. Ask what action would naturally follow if that knowing were real.
3. Locate the obstruction: desire, fear, habit, laziness, social pressure.
4. Redefine the work as removing obstruction, not adding slogans.

**Trade-offs**: powerful for self-cultivation; should be used with compassion, not accusation.

## 格物 in the Affair

**When to use**: when learning has become abstract.

**How**:
1. Find where 意 is directed.
2. Treat that directed affair as 物.
3. Ask what is not upright in this intention.
4. Rectify there, not in a fantasy situation.

**Trade-offs**: keeps practice concrete; may feel less intellectually impressive than theory.

## 读书返身

**When to use**: studying classics, philosophy, or ethics.

**How**:
1. Read for a principle.
2. Translate it into a present affair.
3. Ask how it exposes your own intention.
4. Test it in conduct.

**Trade-offs**: slower than collecting notes; produces living understanding.

## 事上磨炼

**When to use**: work conflict, family duty, leadership pressure, temptation, or frustration.

**How**:
1. Do not flee the affair merely because it is uncomfortable.
2. Use the affair as the place where selfish desire appears.
3. Clarify the right action.
4. Act and reflect.

**Trade-offs**: prevents quietism; needs rest and discernment so practice does not become compulsive busyness.

## 本体功夫 Balance

**When to use**: when someone either over-theorizes original nature or overworks external routines.

**How**:
1. Remember the root: moral clarity is not imported from outside.
2. Remember the work: obstruction must be removed in action.
3. Tie every insight to one concrete practice.

**Trade-offs**: avoids both empty mysticism and mechanical moralism.
