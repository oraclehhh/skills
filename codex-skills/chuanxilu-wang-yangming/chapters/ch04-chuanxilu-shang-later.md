# Chapter 4: 上卷问答延展

## Core Idea
Later upper-volume dialogues extend the basic doctrine into reading, stillness, emotion, knowledge, and daily affairs. The point is always to establish a living root rather than accumulate external knowledge.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **精一互成**: refinement is the work by which unity is realized; unity is not separate from refinement.
- **静中与事上**: quiet sitting can help, but the test is whether the heart remains clear in affairs.
- **Reading as Self-Return**: books are useful when they return the learner to their own heart and conduct.
- **Rooted Learning**: knowledge grows from the source, not from scattered acquisition.

## Key Concepts
- **陆澄**: questioner in many dialogues on practice and learning.
- **精一**: refined concentration and moral unity.
- **未发之中**: equilibrium before emotions arise, not a detached blankness.
- **读书**: not mere textual comprehension but self-cultivation.

## Mental Models
Use "quiet reveals, affairs verify." Stillness may show the heart's condition, but friction in real life reveals whether it is stable.

## Anti-patterns
- **Bookish accumulation**: reading more to avoid changing oneself.
- **Quietism**: mistaking calm withdrawal for moral achievement.
- **Scattered effort**: treating cultivation as many unrelated tasks.

## Worked Example
If meditation feels clear but anger returns instantly in conflict, the practice has not yet penetrated affairs. Use the conflict itself as the next site of 格物.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reading must return to practice.
2. Stillness and activity are not separate moral worlds.
3. 精一 is achieved through concrete refinement.
