# Chapter 2: 传习录序

## Core Idea
序文 frames the text as transmitted learning: disciples record what they heard and practiced so later learners can enter the same work of clarifying the heart and rectifying conduct.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Transmission Through Practice**: learning is received by hearing, questioning, testing, and embodying.
- **Disciple Stance**: doubt and questioning are allowed, but must return to self-examination.
- **Record as Guidance**: the text preserves teaching moments rather than final scholastic closure.

## Key Concepts
- **传习**: transmission and repeated practice.
- **序**: preface explaining origin, purpose, and stance.
- **门人**: students who record and continue the teaching.

## Mental Models
Read the text as a conversation you join. Ask your own question, then test whether the answer exposes your present obstruction.

## Anti-patterns
- **Passive reverence**: treating the classic as sacred but not actionable.
- **Suspicion without practice**: doubting intellectually without trying the method in conduct.

## Worked Example
A learner puzzled by 知行合一 should not only compare doctrines. They should identify one known duty they avoid, act on it, and then reread the doctrine from inside the experience.

## Key Takeaways
1. Transmission depends on practice.
2. Questions are part of learning.
3. The text is a guide to self-cultivation, not a museum piece.
