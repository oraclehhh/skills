# Glossary

**Anchoring and adjustment**: estimating from an initial value and then insufficiently adjusting away from it (Ch 4).

**Availability heuristic**: judging frequency or likelihood by how easily examples come to mind (Ch 5).

**Base rate**: the prior frequency of an event in a relevant reference class (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Bounded rationality**: rationality constrained by limited information, attention, time, memory, and computation (Ch 1).

**Choice procedure**: the rule that converts preferences and beliefs into an action (Ch 2, Ch 10).

**Conjunction fallacy**: judging a detailed combined event as more likely than one of its broader components (Ch 5, Ch 6).

**Decision tree**: a representation of actions, uncertain events, probabilities, and outcomes (Ch 2, Ch 8, Ch 11).

**Descriptive decision theory**: models of how people actually decide, including systematic deviations from rational norms (Ch 12).

**Ecological validity**: the actual relationship between a cue and the criterion being judged (Ch 3).

**Expected utility**: probability-weighted value of possible outcomes, using subjective utility rather than raw money alone (Ch 11).

**Framing effect**: preference change caused by different descriptions of equivalent options (Ch 9, Ch 12).

**Gambler's fallacy**: belief that an independent random process is "due" to reverse after a streak (Ch 7).

**Heuristic**: a simple cognitive shortcut that can be efficient but may fail under specific conditions (Ch 5).

**Hindsight bias**: seeing an outcome as more predictable after knowing it happened (Ch 6).

**Lens model**: a framework comparing environmental cue validity with human cue utilization (Ch 3).

**Linear model**: a weighted additive model for predicting or judging outcomes from cues (Ch 3).

**Loss aversion**: losses loom larger than equivalent gains in many choices (Ch 12).

**Preference construction**: the idea that preferences are often formed during elicitation rather than simply retrieved (Ch 9, Ch 10).

**Probability tree**: a branching representation of sequential uncertain events, useful for conditional probability (Ch 8).

**Regression to the mean**: extreme observations tend to be followed by less extreme observations when noise is present (Ch 7).

**Representativeness heuristic**: judging likelihood by similarity to a prototype or stereotype (Ch 5).

**Sample space**: the complete set of possible outcomes under consideration (Ch 8).

**Story construction**: forming a coherent causal narrative to organize evidence and make judgments (Ch 6).

**Sunk cost**: a cost already incurred that should not affect future choice if it cannot be recovered (Ch 2, Ch 12).

**Utility**: subjective value of an outcome, including but not limited to money (Ch 9, Ch 11).
