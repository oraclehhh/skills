# Chapter 10: 从偏好到选择

## Core Idea

偏好不会自动产生选择。人们需要某种选择程序，而程序本身会影响最终答案。

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Ranking Procedure**: order options by comparative preference.
- **Weighted Additive Choice**: score options across attributes and weights.
- **Noncompensatory Rules**: eliminate options that fail critical criteria.
- **Constructed Preference**: preferences may be formed during the choice task.

## Key Concepts

- **Attribute**: dimension of comparison.
- **Trade-off**: sacrificing one attribute for another.
- **Lexicographic rule**: choose by the most important attribute first.
- **Satisficing**: choose an option that clears a threshold.

## Mental Models

Use "procedure explains preference." If a person's choice seems inconsistent, inspect whether the elicitation method changed from rating to ranking to pricing to forced choice.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming stable hidden preference**: treating every observed choice as retrieval of a pre-existing value.
- **Uncontrolled elicitation**: comparing choices produced by different procedures as if they were equivalent.

## Worked Example

When buying a laptop, one person may rank by battery life, another may eliminate anything above a price cap, and another may use a weighted matrix. Different procedures can choose different machines even with the same facts.

## Key Takeaways

1. Choice procedures shape decisions.
2. Preferences can be constructed in context.
3. Make trade-offs explicit before trusting a final choice.
