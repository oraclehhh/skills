# Chapter 7: 偶然与因果

## Core Idea

人们经常低估随机过程中的聚集、连串和回归现象，于是把偶然误看成因果、趋势或能力变化。

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Randomness Calibration**: random sequences often contain streaks and clusters.
- **Gambler's Fallacy Check**: independent events do not become due for reversal.
- **Regression to the Mean**: extreme observations often move toward average on later measurement.

## Key Concepts

- **Random sequence**: a sequence generated without systematic pattern.
- **Streak**: a run of similar outcomes.
- **Regression**: movement from extreme observed values toward average because of noise.

## Mental Models

Use "mechanism before meaning." Do not infer a cause from a streak unless there is a plausible mechanism and comparison data.

## Anti-patterns

- **Hot-hand certainty**: treating a run of success as proof of changed ability.
- **Punish after extreme failure, reward after extreme success**: confusing regression with intervention effects.

## Worked Example

After an unusually poor performance, improvement next time may occur even without coaching because the first result included bad luck. If coaching happened between the two performances, improvement may be wrongly attributed to coaching unless compared with similar uncoached cases.

## Key Takeaways

1. Randomness looks less even than intuition expects.
2. Extreme cases are likely to regress.
3. Causal claims need mechanisms and counterfactuals.
