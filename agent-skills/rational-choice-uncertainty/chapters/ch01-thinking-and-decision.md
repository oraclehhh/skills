# Chapter 1: 思维和决策

## Core Idea

人类决策不是在无限理性中完成的，而是在有限注意、有限工作记忆、有限时间和有限信息中完成的。理解判断与决策，必须同时看认知系统的能力和局限。

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Bounded Rationality**: use when behavior looks irrational; inspect the cognitive and environmental constraints before judging the person.
- **Cognitive Architecture Constraint**: working memory is limited, so people simplify, chunk, attend selectively, and use procedures.
- **Decision Procedure View**: a decision is not only the final choice; it is the procedure by which alternatives, evidence, and values are processed.

## Key Concepts

- **Working memory**: the limited mental workspace in which active reasoning happens.
- **Hypothesis testing**: people often test candidate explanations rather than passively absorb evidence.
- **Procedural rationality**: the quality of the process, not only the optimality of the result.

## Mental Models

Use a cognitive-bottleneck model when a decision maker ignores information. The missing information may not be rejected; it may never have fit into the active problem representation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Outcome-only rationality**: judging the quality of thinking only by whether the outcome was good.
- **Unlimited mind assumption**: designing decisions as if people can compare all variables at once.

## Worked Example

For a complex purchase, a fully rational comparison would evaluate every option on every attribute. A bounded decision maker narrows the set, focuses on a few dimensions, uses defaults, and may stop when one option is good enough. The practical question is whether the simplification preserves the most diagnostic criteria.

## Key Takeaways

1. Start analysis with the decision maker's information and cognitive constraints.
2. Simplification is inevitable; the design goal is better simplification.
3. Rationality can be evaluated at the level of procedures, not only outcomes.
