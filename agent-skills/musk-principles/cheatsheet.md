# Cheatsheet: Musk Principles

## Decision Rules

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| You are choosing what to work on | Pick the useful, high-leverage problem over the prestigious one |
| The industry says something is impossible | Break it into first principles and identify the actual limit |
| The schedule feels comfortable | Compress it until the bottleneck becomes visible |
| A process is complex | Delete before optimizing; automate last |
| A team is slow | Reduce handoffs, clarify ownership, move decisions closer to builders |
| A prototype works but scale fails | Treat manufacturing as the product |
| People avoid hard feedback | Protect truth and standards over comfort |
| A company lacks direction | Tie strategy to a mission large enough to guide trade-offs |
| A plan relies on one path | Run parallel attempts where failure is cheap and time matters |
| A risk threatens civilization | Treat it as a design constraint, not background anxiety |

## Work Algorithm

1. Make the goal explicit.
2. Identify the useful output.
3. Question every requirement.
4. Delete unnecessary parts, meetings, approvals, and features.
5. Simplify what remains.
6. Move the work to builders with direct ownership.
7. Compress the cycle time.
8. Test in reality.
9. Fix the weakest link.
10. Automate only after the process is proven.

## Team Smells

| Smell | Likely Problem | Countermove |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone agrees in meetings | Fear or low standards | Ask for direct objections |
| Many coordinators, few builders | Process bloat | Move authority to makers |
| Requirements never questioned | Inherited assumptions | Ask who owns each requirement |
| Prototype celebrated too early | Manufacturing ignored | Build production plan in parallel |
| Slow feedback cycles | Organization too indirect | Put responsible people in the same loop |
| Deadlines always expand | No urgency pressure | Set aggressive intermediate milestones |

## First-Principles Prompt

Ask:
- What are we assuming because others do it this way?
- What are the physical, economic, or logical constraints?
- What is the theoretical floor for cost, time, mass, energy, or complexity?
- Which requirement can be deleted?
- What would the solution look like if we started from zero?

## Future-Back Filter

Prioritize projects that:
- Increase useful capability.
- Reduce scarcity.
- Improve energy, transport, manufacturing, compute, or knowledge.
- Protect long-term human flourishing.
- Can become a compounding platform, not just a one-off product.
